Case Study

A weekly Grocery Membership to a shop anytime Grocery Store

Misfits Market

Role: Lead Product Designer

Team: Lead Product Designer, Project Manager, QA Analyst, Software Engineers

Tools: Figma, Trymyui, Asana, Slack

Touchpoints: Storefront, Aisles, Cart, Account & Dashboard

The Goal

Misfits Market wanted to expand assortment and capture more of a grocery shopper's basket by allowing customers to add on meat, seafood, and other cold items to their order

Goal Definition

1 - Allow users to add cold items on 'Our Picks', the marketplace home

Merchandizing wanted the ability for users to start their pack on the storefront home without leaving the homepage.

2 - Design a centralized location for all kinds of products that need to be shipped in a cold pack

Create one aisle hub for all items that can be shipped in a cold pack anything from pies to bacon to more.

3 - Allow for aisles to have both normal products and products that need to be shipped in a cold pack

Design an experience with aisles that contain cold items and non cold items.

Success Indicators or KPIs: Average Order Value of Pack (items that need to be shipped cold), Percentage of users ordering cold packs

Limitations: The current experience redirected users to the meat & seafood page, aka the cold aisle, to build their pack. They had to complete their pack on the meat & seafood page in order to add it to cart or even to view it in cart. If they navigated away from the page the system would remember their picks so when a user returned to the page they continue where they left off. It was a frustrating experience for the user that was created because of limited technological infrastructure.

Research & Ideation

First I conducted research and created a deck. I presented it to stakeholders to get feedback, narrow focus, and align on a direction to move forward. Below is a sampling of the deck.

Then I organized and lead a workshop. I created a figjam file with thought starters and relevant information (the brief, best practice information, 3 proposed solutions).

By the end of the session there was a clear consensus and a direction forward. It was easy moving forward to creating wireframes, laying out the content, and then forward to creating a prototype for testing.

Testing & Validation

Remote Unmoderated Testing on trymyui.com

5 testers were asked to interact with a prototype of the cold pack experience. They were asked to add a series of items and complete their cold pack.

This test was made with the goal of understanding whether or not the testers conceptually understood the difference between the cold pack cart and the normal cart, the order minimums for each, how to complete a cold pack, and where to go to review them.

  • Take a moment to scroll up and down the page without tapping anything. In your own words, please explain what you are seeing.
  • In your own words please describe how a "cold pack" works.
  • On this page, attempt to add an “apple” to your order. Please explain any changes you are seeing along the way.
  • On this page, attempt to add a “beef” item to your order. Please explain any changes you are seeing along the way.
  • Now imagine you wish to review your order. How would you go about doing so? Please explain what you are seeing along the way.
  • In your own words, what would you expect to happen with your current items in the cart?
  • How would you go about reviewing what's in your cold pack?
  • If you landed on the “Cold Pack Aisle Page” Please explain what you are seeing.
  • How would you go about completing your cold pack?
  • Now imagine you wish to review your order again. How would you go about doing so? Please explain any changes you see.

Analyzing The Findings

Writing the usertesting script and setting up the usertest on Trymyui.com

Some testers were confused how the order minimums worked

One tester thought the order minimums were separate meaning they would need to spend $60 to order non cold pack items and cold pack items. Another tester thought there was just one order minimum $30. Since the business was firm on keeping both order minimums the same amount, I had to really lean on material (form factor), copy, and location (proximity) to really clearly communicate the unique order minimums in addition to separating the locations and access points to the normal cart and cold pack cart, also only adding the cold pack to cart once the minimum was complete.

Two testers failed to locate their cold pack cart

Action Item: include multiple access points to cold pack in several high activity locations such as in the normal cart, on the storefront homepage, and on the cold aisle page.

Iteration & Design

The final cold pack experience

Cold Aisle & Pack

Once on cold Aisle users can view the contents of their pack, track their progress, and see how much they need to add to complete it. Once the pack is added to cart they can expand the component to see exactly what is in their pack and how much each item costs.

Add Anywhere: Users can add cold items from the storefront and from any aisle page. Users can start cold pack anywhere on the storefront now. Whereas before, they could only build their pack on the Meat & Seafood page.

Multiple Entry Ways & Reminders: Users receives storefront disappearing toasts and in cart banners reminding them to finish pack. These toasts and banner also acts as a direct link/entrance into the cold aisle.

Results

Increase in AOV or Average Order Value. Increase in prospective customer interest and engagement on marketing materials featuring cold items. Increase in storefront engagement.
