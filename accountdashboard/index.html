Case Study

Account & Member Dashboard

Misfits Market — 2020

Brand

Misfits Market

Launch Date

July 2020

What

Workshop, moderated user testing, unmoderated user testing, competitive research, best practice research, wireframes, flows, prototype, high fidelity mocks & handoff documentation, visual & interaction QA.

Role

Product Designer (Sole)

Define

Misfits Market wanted to overhaul and update their account experience for three main reasons:

1. To reflect the new subscription structure. Currently: Users can choose between a number of shopping and delivery days. Charges are made on a rolling basis. Before: Users have one shopping and delivery option, and all customers segmented by zipcode are charged on the same day.

2. To improve current customer experience pain points. Currently: Users are confused how to enter the storefront (base box customization and marketplace), how to view past orders, how to change account details, how to get help.

3. To set up account experience to incorporate future growth initiatives. Such as loyalty, referrals, promotions.

KPIs

  • Account page feedback score
  • Volume of CS inquiries about account issues / clarity
  • % of customers who cancel because of poor experience / website was confusing

Jobs To Be Done

  • View current order status and get relevant info.
  • Understand how to shop the customization / marketplace storefronts
  • Manage their upcoming orders
  • Manage their subscription
  • Contact customer service
  • View/manage account details

Research & Ideation

I ran a workshop with stakeholders to share research and brainstorm / ideate ideas. Then voted and narrowed the ideas down to the best ones. The best ideas became the core features.

The core feature ideas that came out of the workshop:

Countdown Timer: Clear understanding when customization and marketplace is open and when it is closed. And clear understanding of how much time user has until it opens.

Order Progress: Clear sense of box journey, where user's box is on its path from factory to user's home (picks made, charged, shipped, delivered). Emphasis on the current order (aka the most relevant one to the user).

Dashboard Interface: A user's misfits market “home” that feels personalized and not like a generic information page.

Testing & Validation with Customer Service

Since customer service was talking to customers on a daily basis and knew their problems first hand, and Marketing & Legal were uncomfortable with product speaking directly to customers, I took them through the prototypes to get their feedback on what was working and what wasn't based on their deep knowledge of customers' biggest complaints.

Key Findings: “Users are always asking how to manage their account, how do they change their shipping delivery day and so forth.” —MM Customer Service Rep. Clarifying the FAQs and updating them with relevant answers to popular questions.

“We hear a lot of users have trouble finding information on how to get help or only after searching for x hrs did they find the link to contact us.” —MM Customer Service Rep. Updating the help & contact-us modules. Positioning them higher on the page.

Validation with Unmoderated Remote User Testing

After we tested the wires on customer service I was able to move forward with the clearer direction and create a polished prototype for user testing. Overall the experience performed well, with one major finding.

Key Findings — Insight: Testers found the page too long to scroll through. Action: Condense and consolidate content. Aim for 3 scrolls maximum on mobile.

A screenshot of the user test conducted on trymyui.com

The Iterated Design

The core features: countdown clock, order progress, friendlier dashboard interface.

Countdown Clock until Storefront Opens

Using gamification principles to encourage customer excitement and anticipation. Sample sale sites use a similar tactic. Also helps user understand in a intuitive way when to shop and when to rest. Previously it displayed the date and users frequently were confused and stressed out about when to shop.

Friendlier Dashboard Interface

The interface got a full makeover. Since moving off of shopify there was increased capabilities and allowed me to create a more modern and welcoming look and feel.

Incorporating the user's first name personalized the experience.

Arranging the content into cards made it feel more dynamic and modern. Additional friendly improvements included highlighting relevant helpful information in the form of a 'Need Help' component with links to relevant FAQs and to contacting customer service.

Separating out account management functions 'My Plan' from viewing past orders 'My Orders' by user want 'I want to change my delivery day (account management)' vs 'I want to view my last order (order history)' created a better wayfinding experience.

Order Progress

Where a box was in its journey was clearly communicated with a progress stepper. Also the most pertinent information a user would want to see was organized in the Current Box section.

Handoff Documentation

Annotated flows, components, and state sheets.

Results

The new account experience launched to employees first and then rolled out to a segment of customers.

After a month of close monitoring the experience

  • Increased engagement (Logins)
  • Increased weekly engagement (users shopping on storefront)
  • Decreased number of Customer Service tickets
  • Increase in user sentiment (Online survey)
