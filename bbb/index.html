Case Study

Increasing conversion through rethinking sign-up

www.misfitsmarket.com/sign-up

Role: Lead Product Designer

Date: 2021

Client: Misfits Market

Date: August 2021 - September 2021

Teams Involved: Customer Experience / Product, Brand / Marketing, Procurement, Merchandizing, Engineering, Operations

My Role: Product Designer (Sole)

Platform: Web - Primary, Mobile - Secondary

What is Misfits Market?

Misfits Market is a direct to consumer grocery delivery service that sells “ugly” food at a discount that conventional grocery stores will not take because of imperfections or deviations from the normal. Or more simply, Misfits Market is the Odd-Lots of e-grocers. Misfits Market started as a box subscription in 2018 and rapidly tripled in size during Covid, raising $16.5 million expanding into a grocery membership.

Defining the Target User

Prospective User Profile

I worked with my project manager to construct a profile for the type of prospective users to target. Majority of prospective users are young educated women who like to eat healthy, like to cook, have favorite products (or a grocery list/range of foods) they like to stick to, and want to get items they are already buying at better prices.

The Current Experience

Prospective customers were arriving on the homepage tapping on get started seeing the information they needed to enter and promptly leaving. There was general information about what percentage discounts they would get but no real specific information on what products they could buy.

Currently prospective users had to sign up and enter both their credit card information and personal information before they could even see products and shop. As you could assume, this caused a lot of friction in acquisition. Users would get to the homepage tap on ‘Get Started,’ see all the form fields they would need to enter, and promptly leave. The “Build Before Buy” concept would allow prospective users to explore Misfits Market’s products & prices, shop, and fill their cart before they sign up, or put more simply allow prospective users to build their box before they buy their box.

The current Sign Up experience made prospective users take 11+ steps before seeing any actual prices or products. Making them sign up, create an account, and even enter payment information before completely understanding what they are buying. Drop off was very high.

Ideation

Process to the solution

Cross-functional Collaboration

Because this redesign involved the homepage, it became a high stakes project. Many teams had different and sometimes contradicting opinions.

Standstill Over The Starting Screen (Homefront/Homepage): Procurement (the team that procures the produce and products) didn’t want to show price information before users signed up because brands (their BFFs) were concerned about other stores finding out and demanding the same prices from them and they would loose profit. Also if Misfits Market’s competitors started matching prices, MM would loose its main competitive advantage: its prices. So essentially the business was split on this issue and kept on going back and forth, and the project was in a standstill. It made it very difficult to design. Wanting to just keep the project moving, I proposed a few solutions/options for the starting screen:

Balancing Multiple Teams’ Agendas: The marketing/brand team wanted to keep the current homepage and all of its marketing content and messaging that they created. But users were use to getting to a grocery site and shopping, they were more convinced by e-commerce or shopping components that they could explore with, than marketing components (messaging, large header photos, illustrations) that they could just look at. So to satisfy them and my team, I took Marketing’s messaging and ideas from the homepage, and translated it into a shopper friendly format: specific product and category components that communicated the same thing.

Alignment: The teams aligned on Solution 1

Solution 1: An emphasis on deals in a natural format: displaying products in their natural product card form displaying a photo, the name, and percentage off but omitting brand and price information.

Solution 2: An emphasis on great prices: displaying evergreen products (products we have on a consistent basis) with their prices but omitting brand names.

Solution 3: An emphasis on overall benefits re-using some of the marketing messaging and content from the current homepage.

Limitations

There were two key limitations: 1) Mandatory Shopping Windows - Technical back-end infrastructure setup in the subscription days that helped the business control supply & demand and forecast earnings. Only the customer facing UI could be changed. 2) Unpredictable Inventory - functioning like a grocery “Odd Lots,” product inventory was different week to week, making it hard to advertise products.

Prototyping the Solution

Moderated User Testing

Number of Testers: 5

Test Length: 45 min

Tools Used: Zoom, Figma, Google Meetings, Google Sheets, Google Docs

Creating a rainbow sheet for analysis

Key Findings & Design Iterations

Summary: Overwhelmingly all testers wanted to get a concrete sense of what products we are selling before entering any of their information. In general, testers scrolled right past the hero and value props until they were specifically asked questions about it. Most found the marketing content the least helpful and the product cards the most helpful. All testers did not get a concrete sense of how Misfits Market helps with food waste, which consequently cut its effectiveness. The one value prop a few testers noted did incentivize/motivate them to sign up was Free Membership which correlates with Price being the biggest incentivizer to sign up followed by Assortment.

Majority of testers fell into two categories: the grocery list writers who favor search bars, and the browsers who favor scrolling through all items. The listers want to see MM has at least 5 items on their list before signing up. Convenience of only having to shop at one site rather than multiple is a big incentivizer. The browsers want to scroll see all the items with prices and mentally compare those prices to a store they frequently shop at (i.e. Trader Joes) to evaluate if its a good deal for them. Deeming value is a big incentivizer. 1 browser in particular disliked and felt overwhelmed by the combination of vertical and horizontal scrolling the carousels required to browse and favored scrolling in one direction (vertical, the most natural) in grid/list format.

Price As King

“Free membership…Obviously price wise the 40% off is important and the prices.” — MK

Assortment as King

“I would automatically go to shop by aisle or all items to look at product selection. Aka would by pass this page and go right to the storefront.” — AB on the homepage

Desire for search to deem assortment

“I want a search bar I don’t want to search by aisle.” — NS on storefront

Percentages Are Not Prices

Testers want to experience the value not read the value. For most testers, percentages were not enough to convince them to sign up and were not a replacement for prices. “Up to 40% off grocery store prices I don’t care for, because its percentage doesn’t mean anything to me. I compare it myself.” — NS

The need to deem good prices and good assortment to order

“After I see what I can get and eyeball the prices that is where I can make the decision on whether or not I want to make an order.” — MK

Category examples to deem assortment

“I would like to see the aisle / categories out in the carousel format.” — AM

Final Design Features

Checkout / Enter Payment Information After Shopping

FROM: Prospective users entering payment information before even seeing prices or products.

TO: Prospective users Create an Account —> Shop (build box) —> Checkout (enter payment & shipping information)

Product and Category Carousels on Homepage

Testers wanted to see what categories of products MM offered so I included a carousel of our categories or ‘aisles’ in the same format that a shopping user was use to.

FROM: Banners with staged photography and general offering messaging but no price information or actual products.

TO: Category / aisle carousel, product cards, product carousels with a hero image on the Homepage to give prospective users enough information to want to create an account and shop.

Sticky Headers and Buttons

FROM: Zipcode field not on homepage, buried on a secondary page. Headers that are only accessible at top of page.

TO: Enter zipcode is now fixed and included in the main header so it is always accessible. The “Access Now” button is now in a sticky bottom bar so it follows the user and is always readily accessible.

Hero Carousel

FROM: A very long homepage filled with several messaging and vague product benefit components.

TO: To come to some kind of compromise to get marketing onboard with the new experience, I turned the Hero section into a carousel that would cycle through the 4 main banners and messaging concepts from the old homepage.

Cart Timeout

FROM: Cart only accessible after signing up for a membership and entering credit card and personal information.

TO: Added a countdown timer in cart, that would hold the products for the prospective customer for a set amount of time. When the time ran out their cart would be emptied giving another user or current user a chance to get those products.

Because of the way our charge scheduler and system works, when a current user adds enough products to their cart to meet the order minimum, those products are held for the customer. This is because in the past when there were shopping windows that opened at 4pm, by 5pm many products were already sold out.

Results

The experience was launched to a segment of prospective customers in September 2021. It was a great success and Misfits Market saw sizable improvements in customer acquisition. It is set to roll out to more customers in the coming months and it will be fully rolled out to all customers by mid to end 2022.
